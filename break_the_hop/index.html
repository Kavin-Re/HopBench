Break the Hop

A frequency-hopping radio jumps between 13 channels. Can you predict the next one? Simulation only: no radio is involved, everything runs in this page and works offline.

How this works

Each slot the transmitter hops to one of 13 channels, chosen by a schedule generator. You see the past hops and click the channel you expect next. A pure guesser is right 1 time in 13 (7.7 %). Three schedules to try:

Weak: LFSR-16 is a 16-bit shift register. Its whole secret is a 16-bit state (65,535 possibilities). Weak: LCG-24 is a linear congruential generator with a 24-bit state. Keyed: SipHash-2-4 hashes the slot number with a 128-bit secret key.

All three look random to a person. The button Reveal the attack runs a breaker that knows the algorithm but not the secret and keeps only the secrets consistent with the hops seen so far.

The first 20 hops are shown for free so you have something to study. Channel labels 1 to 13 correspond to channel indices 0 to 12 in the HopBench Python package.

The attack

Limits: this is a classroom toy. The breaker is one specific attack; failing against it does not prove the keyed schedule is secure, and real radios add timing, synchronisation and RF effects that are not modelled here. Part of the HopBench project (simulation and education only).
